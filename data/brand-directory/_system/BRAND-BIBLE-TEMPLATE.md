# Brand Bible Template

One document per brand. Every caption, headline, ad line, story frame, reply, alt
text and image brief for that brand is checked against it before it ships.

**How it is built**

| Part | What it is | Who fills it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Language Standard | The rules of written English, tone and manners. Pre-written. Identical for every brand. | Nobody. It is binding as is. A brand may tighten a rule in Part 2, never loosen it. |
| 2. The Brand Bible | Everything that makes this brand itself: names, voice, words, facts, lanes, channels, looks. | One owner per brand, signed off by the approver. |
| 3. The Gate | The checklist and scoring that anything runs through before it goes out. | Whoever writes or reviews the copy, human or agent. |
| 4. Machine rules | A small block of settings a linter can read. Copied from Part 2, section 2. | Generated from section 2. |

**Order of precedence when rules disagree**

1. The law and platform terms.
2. Verified facts in the Proof bank (section 7). A fact beats a nice line.
3. Part 1, the Language Standard.
4. The brand's own Part 2 settings.
5. The measured feed (section 16). It informs the bible. It never overrides rules 1 to 4.
6. A campaign brief. A brief may change what is said, never how well it is written.

**Field status.** Every filled field carries one of these, so nobody builds on a guess.

- `CONFIRMED` : the approver has said yes, or it comes from a source named beside it.
- `DRAFT` : written by someone, not yet approved. Usable, flagged in review.
- `UNKNOWN` : nobody knows yet. The gate treats it as "do not say it".

**What counts as a source.** A named document with a date, a link, a file path, a
measurement from the feed audit, or a person with a date. "I think" is not a source.

# PART 1. THE LANGUAGE STANDARD

*The principal's rules.* Imagine the head of a very good school reading your caption
over your shoulder. Not a stern one. A fair one, who notices everything and will not
let a sloppy line through because it was posted in a hurry. Every rule has an ID so a
reviewer can write "fails L5.6" instead of "feels off".

## L1. Spelling and language

- **L1.1** One language variant per brand, recorded in section 2. Never mix variants in a
  post. The default for South African brands is South African English, which follows
  British spelling: colour, centre, programme, tyre, practise (verb), licence (noun),
  `-ise` endings.
- **L1.2** Names are spelled exactly as the registry in section 3 spells them. Brand,
  product, person, venue, partner, handle. `TrackMan`, never `Trackman`. If a name is
  not in the registry, find out how its owner writes it before using it.
- **L1.3** No typos, ever. Read every post once more after the last edit, because the last
  edit is where typos live.
- **L1.4** No text-speak: no `u`, `ur`, `4` for for, `gonna`, `wanna`, `lol`. A quoted customer
  is the only exception, and the quote must be real and permitted.
- **L1.5** No invented words, no pun that needs a footnote, no deliberate misspelling for
  style, unless the brand registry lists it as a signature.

## L2. Grammar and punctuation

- **L2.1** Full sentences. A fragment is allowed only on purpose: one per post at most,
  and it must be doing a job (a hook, a sign-off). A fragment by accident is a mistake.
- **L2.2** **No em dashes and no en dashes in running copy.** Use a full stop, a colon, a comma
  or a pipe. Ranges are written with "to" (10 to 15) or a plain hyphen between numerals.
  This is already the rule on every brand in this repo.
- **L2.3** Apostrophes: `it's` is "it is", `its` is possession. Plurals never take an
  apostrophe (iron sets, not iron's). Use one apostrophe style throughout a post.
- **L2.4** Exclamation marks follow the cap in section 2. The default is none in headlines,
  one at most per post, and only for a real occasion. Two in a row is never right.
- **L2.5** Ellipses are not used for suspense or trailing off. Three dots at the end of a
  line mean the writer ran out of idea.
- **L2.6** Capitalisation: sentence case for headlines and captions. Proper nouns are
  capitalised. ALL CAPS only where a template defines a label, and never more than three
  words in running text. No Random Title Case. No capital letters for emphasis.
- **L2.7** The serial comma follows the brand setting. Whichever is chosen is used every time.
- **L2.8** Lists are parallel: every item starts the same way (all verbs, or all nouns). Do not
  mix "Book a bay" with "Coaching" in the same list.
- **L2.9** One space after a full stop. No double spaces, no trailing spaces, no stray blank
  lines at the end, no line breaks in the middle of a sentence unless it is a designed
  line break.
- **L2.10** Quotation marks follow the brand setting (single or double), and punctuation
  goes where the brand's style puts it. Never quote a customer or an expert without
  permission and a source.
- **L2.11** Abbreviations: spell out on first use unless the audience says it daily. No
  abbreviation that is only common inside the team.

## L3. Numbers, units, money, dates, times

- **L3.1** Numerals for data (carry 145, 4 bays, 30 minutes). Words for one to nine in plain prose.
  Never start a sentence with a numeral.
- **L3.2** Currency format follows section 2 and matches the booking engine and website. Never
  write a price in two formats in one post.
- **L3.3** One unit system per post, never mixed (yards or metres, not both). A figure always
  carries its unit.
- **L3.4** Dates and times follow section 2. A date without a year is acceptable only inside the
  current year. "This Saturday" is allowed in a caption only if the post goes out that
  week. Evergreen copy never uses relative time.
- **L3.5** Every number comes from the Proof bank (section 7) with a source and a date. No
  rounding up to flatter. No "up to" used to hide the typical case. No number that
  looks like a TrackMan or fitting result unless it is a real, permitted one.

## L4. Claims and truth

- **L4.1** If it is not in the Proof bank, do not say it. Prices, hours, stock, brands carried,
  offers, awards, counts, "first", "only", "best". `UNKNOWN` means silent.
- **L4.2** Superlatives and comparatives need evidence a stranger could check. "Premium",
  "world-class" and "leading" are not evidence.
- **L4.3** An offer states four things: what it is, who it is for, when it ends, how to claim it.
  Missing any of the four is an unfinished offer.
- **L4.4** Competitors are never named in a negative light. Fair comparison on a fact is
  allowed only with a source and approver sign-off.
- **L4.5** Partner and supplier names are written the way the partner writes them. Tagging a
  partner follows the permission rule in section 3.
- **L4.6** People in images and quotes: consent is on file, or it is not published.
- **L4.7** Required legal or ad-policy copy is added by the template or the ad setup, never
  left to the writer's memory.

## L5. Caption anatomy

A caption is built, not poured. The parts, in order:

1. **Hook** : line one. It must stand on its own and make sense with the image hidden.
2. **Body** : one idea, one or two short paragraphs.
3. **Proof or detail** : the fact that makes the idea believable (from the Proof bank).
4. **Call to action** : one, exactly.
5. **Sign-off** : only if the brand has a house sign-off (section 6).
6. **Tags** : hashtags and mentions, last.

- **L5.1** The hook is not the brand name, not "We", not a greeting ("Happy Monday"), not a
  question the reader can answer "no" to and scroll on. It opens on the subject, the
  fact, the tension or the reader. Maximum length is the brand setting in section 2,
  set so the whole hook shows before the platform's "more". Check the current platform
  cut-off rather than trusting a remembered number.
- **L5.2** One idea per caption. Two ideas make two posts.
- **L5.3** Length stays inside the band for the lane (section 8). Short is not a virtue by itself.
  Right-sized is.
- **L5.4** Short paragraphs: one or two sentences, a blank line between blocks, no block longer
  than three lines on a phone. A wall of text is a defect.
- **L5.5** The caption adds what the image cannot. It never repeats the on-image text word for
  word. If the image says it, the caption goes further or goes quiet.
- **L5.6** Exactly one call to action. It starts with a verb. It is taken from the brand's
  approved list in section 6 and tells the reader precisely how ("DM us", "link in bio",
  a URL). Two CTAs split the reader. Zero CTAs waste the post, unless the lane is
  declared "no CTA" in section 8.
- **L5.7** The house sign-off, if there is one, is the last line before tags, spelled exactly,
  and never appears mid-caption.
- **L5.8** Hashtags follow the section 2 budget: count, placement, CamelCase for multi-word tags
  (`#GolfFitting`, not `#golffitting`), brand tags first. None in stories text. A hashtag
  never replaces a word in a sentence.
- **L5.9** Emoji follow the budget and the allowed list in section 2. Never as a bullet, never
  in place of a word, never in the hook unless the brand allows it, never a cluster.
- **L5.10** Mentions use verified handles, spelled and capitalised as the account has them. Tag
  only when the registry says tagging is welcome.
- **L5.11** Links: Instagram captions do not carry live links, so they say "link in bio". Where a
  URL is shown, it is complete, correct, lowercase, without tracking junk.
- **L5.12** Alt text is a plain, literal description of what is in the image, written for
  someone who cannot see it. No keyword stuffing. No "image of".
- **L5.13** On-image headlines stay inside the template's limit (section 14) and read correctly
  without the caption.

## L6. Register and manners

- **L6.1** **Precision over adjectives.** Every sentence carries a fact, a feeling or an action. If
  deleting it loses nothing, delete it.
- **L6.2** **The swap test.** Replace the brand name with a competitor's. If the line still works,
  it says nothing. Rewrite it with something only this brand can say.
- **L6.3** Plain words. Choose the shorter word. Write so a sharp 14-year-old can follow the
  sentence the first time. Jargon is used only if the audience uses it, and is
  explained the first time with the plain conclusion beside it.
- **L6.4** Active voice, present tense, second person. "You" is the reader. "We" is used only
  for something the team actually does, never for "our passion" or "our commitment".
- **L6.5** Confident, not loud. State it. No hedging (just, maybe, kind of, we think). No
  shouting (amazing, incredible, insane).
- **L6.6** Respect the reader. Never mock the player's ability. Humour is shared, never aimed.
  No guilt, no shaming, no fear used as a lever.
- **L6.7** Manners: a plain "please" and "thank you" where they are earned. Replies address the
  person, not the post.
- **L6.8** Same voice across a lane. The voice dial settings in section 5 do not drift from post
  to post. Tone moves with the situation (section 5), never with the writer's mood.
- **L6.9** **Read it aloud.** If you stumble, the reader will. Rewrite until it comes out clean.
- **L6.10** Say it once. No repeated word within a sentence unless it is deliberate, and no
  restating the hook in the closing line.

## L7. Banned patterns (every brand)

These are the fingerprints of lazy or machine-written copy. A brand may add to this
list in section 6, never remove from it.

**Empty words and phrases**

`unlock`, `elevate`, `level up`, `next level`, `game changer`, `game-changing`, `journey`
(as a metaphor), `seamless`, `curated`, `tailored solutions`, `bespoke` (unless literally
made to order), `state-of-the-art`, `world-class`, `premium experience`, `cutting-edge`,
`passionate about`, `your one-stop`, `look no further`, `we've got you covered`,
`take your game to the next level`, `delve`, `dive into`, `in today's fast-paced world`,
`whether you're a ... or a ...`, `crushing it`, `beast mode`

**Filler openers and closers**

`Ready to...?`, `Looking to...?`, `Imagine...`, `Picture this`, `Calling all golfers`,
`Exciting news`, `We are thrilled / delighted / excited to announce`, `Say hello to`,
`Introducing`, `Happy [day]!`, `Stay tuned`, `Don't miss out`, `Hit us up`, `Link below`
(when it is in the bio), `Let that sink in`

**Engagement bait**

`Tag a friend who...`, `Comment YES if...`, `Like if you agree`, `Double tap`, `Share with
someone who...`, giveaways that are really follower farming

**Structures**

- "It's not just X, it's Y." and every cousin of it.
- Three adjectives in a row. Three parallel clauses used only for rhythm.
- A question the caption never answers.
- A colon-and-list "listicle" voice in a caption that should be a sentence.
- Punchy one-word paragraphs stacked for drama.
- Self-congratulation: telling the reader the product is great instead of showing why.
- Anything that sounds like a hotel brochure, a corporate About page or a press release.

**Always wrong**

Em dashes. Double exclamation marks. All-caps sentences. A hashtag wall. An emoji
sandwich. A caption that is only a tagline. A caption that is only a hashtag.

## 8. Caption lanes

A lane is a recurring kind of post. Each gets its own anatomy, length and examples.
Start from the feed's real lanes (section 16), not from the ones in the old bible.

Repeat this block for each lane.

```
LANE: [name]
Purpose:            what this post does for the business
Audience:           which segment
Frequency:          how often, which channels
Anatomy:            which of hook / body / proof / CTA / sign-off are present, in order
Length:             [min] to [max] words, [N] lines
Hook patterns:      three approved ways to open
CTA:                the one approved CTA, with exact "how"
Voice dials:        any lane-specific setting
On-image text:      template id, headline limit
Approved example:   a real post, annotated
Rejected example:   a real or plausible post, with the rule IDs it breaks
Notes:              anything special
```

Minimum lanes for a retail and service brand: product arrival, service explainer,
education, offer, event or booking push, member or customer story, behind the
scenes, celebration or milestone, local or seasonal.

# PART 3. THE GATE

Everything runs through it. Captions, headlines, ad copy, stories, reel scripts and
on-screen text, replies, emails, alt text, image briefs, template fields.

## Step 1. Set the context

Write these four lines first. If you cannot, stop and find out.

```
Brand:   ______   Lane: ______   Channel: ______   Asset: ______
```

## Step 2. Load only what applies

| Asset | Load |
|---|---|
| Caption | Part 1 in full; sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 |
| Headline or on-image text | L1, L2, L3, L4, L6, L7; sections 2, 3, 6, 14 |
| Story frame | L1, L2, L6, L7; sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 9 |
| Reel script and on-screen text | Part 1; sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9 |
| Paid ad copy | Part 1 in full; sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9; platform ad policy |
| Reply, DM, review response | L1, L2, L6, L7; sections 3, 5.3, 6, 10 |
| Email, WhatsApp, SMS | Part 1; sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9 |
| Alt text | L1, L2, L5.12; section 3 |
| Image or video brief | Sections 11, 12, 13, 14; L4.6 |

## Step 3. Write, then check

Write the draft. Then run the checks in this order, because the cheap ones catch
the most.

### Hard fails. Any one means rewrite, no score.

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| A fact, number, price, offer or claim not in the Proof bank | L3.5, L4.1 |
| A name misspelled or a partner used without permission | L1.2, L4.5 |
| An em dash or en dash | L2.2 |
| A banned word or pattern from L7 or the brand list | L7 |
| More than one CTA, or a CTA outside the approved list | L5.6 |
| Typo, text-speak or wrong language variant | L1 |
| Exclamation marks over the cap | L2.4 |
| Hook over the character limit, or hook is a greeting or the brand name | L5.1 |
| Copy over the template's declared limit | section 14 |
| A person, product or place shown that is not real or not cleared | L4.6, section 12 |
| Offer missing any of what, who, when, how | L4.3 |

### Scored checks. Score each 0, 1 or 2.

0 = fails, 1 = acceptable, 2 = good enough to use as an example.

| # | Dimension | What a 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Stands alone, specific, earns the second line |
| 2 | One idea | Nothing in the caption could be its own post |
| 3 | Specificity | Passes the swap test (L6.2) |
| 4 | Anatomy | Parts in order, lengths in the lane's band |
| 5 | Voice | Matches the dial settings and the situation row |
| 6 | Plain English | Reads cleanly aloud, shortest word each time |
| 7 | Layout | Short paragraphs, no wall, tags last and few |
| 8 | CTA | Verb first, exact how, matches the lane |

**Pass: 12 or more out of 16, with no 0.** Anything below 12, or with a 0, is rewritten.

## Step 4. Fix and log

Rewrite once, citing the rule IDs, then run the checks again. If it fails a second
time, the problem is the brief, not the wording: go back to the lane, the proof or
the idea.

## Step 5. Record

```

Hard fails:       none | list rule IDs
Score:            _ / 16   (hook _, idea _, specific _, anatomy _, voice _, plain _, layout _, CTA _)
Facts used:       list Proof bank rows
Rules cited in fixes:
Reviewer / date:
Approver (if the lane requires one):
```

## Worked example

**Brand and lane:** a retail-and-service golf brand, product arrival, Instagram feed.

**Draft:**
> 🔥 Exciting news golfers!!! We're thrilled to announce our brand new [Brand] irons are
> finally here — and they are a total game changer for your game! Come check them out
> and unlock your full potential 🏌️‍♂️ Tag a friend who needs new irons! #golf #irons
> #golfclubs #golfer #golflife #newgear #golfaddict

**Gate:**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Hard fail: em dash | yes (L2.2) |
| Hard fail: banned patterns | "Exciting news", "thrilled to announce", "game changer", "unlock your full potential", "Tag a friend" (L7) |
| Hard fail: exclamation over cap | three in a row (L2.4) |
| Hard fail: hook | opens on "Exciting news", a filler opener, then greets the reader (L5.1) |
| Hard fail: CTA | two CTAs, neither says how (L5.6) |
| Hard fail: facts | no model, no price, no stock confirmation (L4.1) |
| Score | would be capped at 0 on hook, specificity, CTA |

**Rewrite** (facts shown as placeholders; every one must come from the Proof bank):
> The [Brand] [model] irons are in stock at [location].
>
> [One true detail the golfer cares about.] [Who they suit.]
>
> DM us to book a fitting before you buy.
>
> [Sign-off if the brand has one]
>
> #[BrandTag] #[LocationTag]

Hook states the news and the place. One idea. Facts are verifiable. One CTA with its
how. Two hashtags in CamelCase. No banned pattern. Score 13 to 15 once the
placeholders are real.

# PART 4. MACHINE RULES

Copy this block into the top of the brand's `BRAND-BIBLE.md`, filled from section 2
and the banned list, so a linter can check copy without reading prose. Keys are fixed
so one linter serves every brand.

```yaml
brand: <brand-id>
language: en-ZA
serial_comma: false
quotes: double
exclamation_max_per_post: 1
exclamation_max_in_headline: 0
hook_max_chars: <N>
hashtags_max: <N>
hashtags_camelcase: true
emoji_max_per_post: <N>
emoji_allowed: []
currency_format: "R1 200"
unit_system: yards
date_format: "D MMMM"
time_format: "HH:mm"
signoff: ""                  # exact text or empty
ctas_allowed: []             # exact approved strings
banned_universal: ref:L7     # linter loads the L7 list
banned_brand: []
names_exact: []              # exact spellings from section 3
names_wrong: []              # known misspellings to flag, e.g. Trackman
lanes: {}                    # lane id -> {min_words, max_words, cta_required, signoff_required}
```

**What a linter can check without judgement:** em and en dashes, banned words and
phrases, exclamation count, emoji count and list, hashtag count and CamelCase, hook
length, CTA count and membership, sign-off position, name spellings, double spaces,
trailing whitespace, ALL CAPS runs, numbers without units, currency format, template
limits.

**What stays with a human:** the swap test, the idea, the tone, whether a fact is
true, whether the humour lands. The scored checks exist for these.

## Using this with Campaign OS

1. Copy Parts 2 and 4 into `data/brand-directory/<brand>/BRAND-BIBLE.md`. Part 1 and Part 3 stay here as the shared standard and are referenced, not copied.
2. Fill sections 0 to 3 and 7 first. Names, settings and facts unlock everything else.
3. Run the feed audit and fill section 16 before writing sections 5, 6 and 8. Write the voice the feed actually has, then decide on purpose where to move it.
4. Point copy generation, reviewers and any agent at the bible as the first thing loaded, ahead of the older `voice/` and `copy/` files, which it supersedes once confirmed.
5. Nothing ships until it has a Gate result.
