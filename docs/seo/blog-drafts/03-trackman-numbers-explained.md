# TrackMan Numbers Explained: What Smash Factor, Attack Angle and Path Mean

TrackMan gives you a lot of numbers. Most golfers only need five of them. Here is what each one means and what it tells you about your swing.

## Club head speed

How fast the club head is moving at impact. It is the main driver of distance. Faster is not always better if the strike gets worse, which is why the next number matters.

## Ball speed and smash factor

Ball speed is how fast the ball leaves the clubface. Smash factor is ball speed divided by club head speed.

It tells you how cleanly you struck the ball. Two golfers with the same club head speed can have very different ball speeds. The gap is strike quality.

## Attack angle

Whether the club is travelling up or down when it meets the ball.

With a driver, hitting slightly up usually helps launch and distance. With an iron, hitting down compresses the ball. Which is right depends on the club. See how [driver loft](/best-driver-loft-for-distance/) interacts with this.

## Club path and face angle

These two numbers explain the shape of the shot.

- **Club path** is the direction the club head is moving through impact.
- **Face angle** is where the clubface is pointing at impact.

Face angle mostly decides where the ball starts. The difference between face and path decides how much it curves. That is why a slice has a cause you can measure and then change.

## Carry distance and dispersion

Carry is how far the ball flies before it lands. Dispersion is how spread out your shots are.

Look at the pattern across a set of shots, not at the best one. A golfer who knows where a typical shot ends can plan a round.

## How to use the numbers

1. **Pick one number.** Not five.
2. **Make one change.** Then hit a set of balls.
3. **Check whether that number moved.** If it did, repeat. If it did not, change something else.

A coach shortens this. They read the numbers and tell you which one matters most for you.

## Why this matters for clubs

The same numbers decide which clubs suit you. A fitting uses your swing speed, attack angle, path and face to match the head, shaft and loft. Read more on [club fitting](/club-fitting/) and why [indoor fittings work](/indoor-vs-outdoor-club-fitting/).
