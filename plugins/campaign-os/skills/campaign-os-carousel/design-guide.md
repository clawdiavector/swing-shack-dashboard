# Carousel design guide

Christelle's art direction for carousels, 27 Oct 2026. Read all of it before designing a
slide. `SKILL.md` has the process, the checks and how to lodge.

A carousel is not a collection of individual posts. It is a sequence, and each slide has
a job. The first slide earns attention. The middle slides reward it. The final slide
gives the viewer somewhere to go.

## 1. The hero slide

Slide 1 is the most important slide. Its job is not to explain everything; its job is
to make someone stop. It has:

- one clear idea
- one dominant image or design move
- very little copy
- strong visual tension
- enough mystery to make people swipe

**Good:** a large Psycho Bunny product image, one strong line (NEW IN, or PSYCHO BUNNY),
a bold colour field pulled from the clothing. Nothing else.

**Bad:** PSYCHO BUNNY / NEW COLLECTION / AVAILABLE NOW / SHIRTS, PANTS, CAPS / DM US /
logo / price / five product benefits. That is a catalogue cover, not a hero.

## 2. Details live in the slides

Don't overload the first frame. The swipe reveals information gradually:

| Slide | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | Attention |
| 2 | What it is |
| 3 | Key product detail |
| 4 | Another detail or use case |
| 5 | Product range or options |
| 6 | Availability, CTA |

It should feel like the carousel is unfolding, not repeating itself.

## 3. Build a colour story

Every carousel has one colour story, intentional from slide 1 to the last frame. Start
with the hero image and pull colours from the product, the venue, the packaging or the
campaign identity. Choose **one dominant colour, one supporting colour, one accent**,
and carry that palette through every slide.

Example: a cream shirt with green detail gives a warm cream base, dark green support,
bright green accent. Don't suddenly bring in orange, blue and red on later slides. The
colour story is what makes several slides feel like one campaign.

## 4. Colour progression

Same palette, used differently on each slide:

| Slide | |
|---|---|
| 1 | Full green background |
| 2 | Cream background, green type |
| 3 | Product image with a green shape |
| 4 | Dark background with cream detail |
| 5 | Green accent returns strongly |
| 6 | Clean cream CTA slide |

Same world, different compositions.

## 5. Each slide needs a role

Never add a slide because the carousel "needs six". Each slide earns its place. Roles:
hero, problem, product, detail, feature, proof, comparison, education, close-up,
lifestyle, range, CTA. Don't repeat a role without a reason.

## 6. Default structures

**Product spotlight**
1. Hero: big visual, minimal copy
2. Introduction: what is it?
3. Detail: something worth noticing
4. Variation: colour, style, model or option
5. Why it matters, kept short
6. CTA: available now; DM, visit or book

**Fitting education**
1. A strong golfer problem or question, e.g. WHY DOES YOUR 7-IRON KEEP GOING LEFT?
2. Simple explanation
3. The fitting variable
4. The data or visual proof
5. What proper fitting changes
6. Book a fitting

Don't turn every slide into a paragraph.

**Campaign spotlight**
1. The campaign idea
2. Mood or product
3. Hero item
4. Second item or detail
5. Range, supporting products
6. Availability

The first frame sells the idea; the rest sells the campaign.

## 7. Create a swipe

A carousel should pull the eye to the next frame. Use lines that continue into the next
frame, shapes cut at the edge, repeated typography, colour shifts, image direction,
objects facing toward the swipe, partial reveals.

Example: a thick green line leaves the right edge of slide 1, and slide 2 begins with
that line entering from the left. Now the carousel feels connected.

## 8. Visual threads

Choose one or two devices and carry them through: one repeating circle, one line system,
one frame, one oversized word, one shadow treatment, one colour block, one typographic
alignment. Don't bring in a new design system on every slide. Consistency comes from
repetition; interest comes from variation.

## 9. Typography across a carousel

Type changes scale without changing personality:

| Slide | Type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Huge, bold |
| 2 | Medium headline |
| 3 | Small detailed information |
| 4 | Huge number or product name |
| 5 | Editorial detail |
| 6 | Clean CTA |

The same headline size and layout on every slide is the "copy paste, copy paste"
problem.

## 10. Big type plus small detail

One of the strongest systems: big emotional type, then small precise information.

1. HERSCHEL, huge.
2. NEW CAMO RANGE / AVAILABLE AT SWING SHACK, small.
3. Close-up product image, tiny product note.

That makes rhythm.

## 11. Use space differently

Some frames feel open, minimal, premium; others tighter, editorial, information-rich.
Don't give every slide the same margins and spacing. Think: quiet, loud, quiet, detail,
hero, close.

## 12. Product image rules

The product stays real. Never recolour it, distort it, invent details, change its
branding or add features it doesn't have.

You may crop it on purpose, scale it hard, layer it behind or in front of type, give it
a realistic shadow, isolate it on colour, or put it in a believable surreal setting. The
design happens around the product.

## 13. The hero can be creative

Slide 1 may be the boldest frame: a giant product with a tiny headline, a giant word
behind the product, one impossible but believable product scene, an oversized shadow,
the product breaking through a frame, a shape-led composition, or the product almost
absent with type carrying the idea. The hero should make the carousel worth opening.

## 14. Don't put everything on slide 1

Hard rule. If slide 1 holds the product, a description, specs, price, CTA,
availability, logo and supporting copy, the carousel has failed before slide 2. Push
information deeper into the sequence.

## 15. Text density

Slide 1 very low; slides 2 to 5 low to medium; the final slide low. No paragraphs
unless the carousel is educational, and even then break it into short lines, numbers,
labels, diagrams and comparisons. The carousel is visual first.

## 16. Story before slide count

Use the number of slides the story needs. Default 4 to 7. If it works in four, stop at
four. Never add filler.

## 17. Final slide

Not THANK YOU. It closes the loop, and the CTA stays simple:

| Kind | Close |
|---|---|
| Retail | NOW AT SWING SHACK |
| Fitting | BOOK YOUR FITTING |
| Campaign | SEE THE RANGE IN STORE |
| Education | STOP GUESSING. GET FITTED. |

## 18. Platform format

Primary format 4:5 portrait. Keep key type away from the edges, product faces and logos
inside the safe area, and important text clear of the app's buttons.

For Google Business or any other 4:3 placement, make a separate hero from slide 1. Don't
blindly crop the 4:5 artwork; recompose it so the idea survives the format change.

## 19. Until multi-image posting exists (T18)

The carousel is delivered as numbered slide files, one ordered manifest, the caption,
alt text if needed, and the 4:3 hero where needed:

```
01_hero.png
02_detail.png
03_product.png
04_proof.png
05_cta.png
```

The order must never depend on file names sorting the wrong way.

## 20. Carousel QA: reject it if

- slide 1 explains too much
- the slides look like separate posts
- the palette changes at random
- the same layout repeats on every frame
- the type hierarchy is identical on every slide
- the product name repeats without need
- later slides repeat slide 1
- there is no visual reason to swipe
- the CTA comes before the story has earned it
- product details are wrong
- the product has been altered
- it feels like a catalogue
- there's no clear colour story
- a detail could be removed without losing anything

## 21. Hero QA

Would this image stop someone without the rest of the carousel? If not, redesign it.
Does it give away too much? If so, take information off. The hero earns attention; the
slides earn understanding.

## 22. Sequence QA

Look at all the slides side by side (the contact sheet):

| Check | Ask |
|---|---|
| Colour | Do they belong to the same world? |
| Rhythm | Does scale and density vary? |
| Movement | Does the eye move left to right? |
| Repetition | Are the visual devices repeated on purpose? |
| Variety | Do the compositions change enough to stay interesting? |
| Story | Does each slide move the idea forward? |

If you remove a slide and nothing changes, that slide was filler.

## 23. Performance learning

Learn from real feed numbers: reach, impressions, saves, shares, comments, profile
visits, swipe completion where available, engagement rate, clicks and enquiries. Split
by brand, content pillar, hero style, slide count, product vs education, type-led vs
image-led, colour-led vs neutral.

Don't conclude "carousels work". Learn which kind of carousel works for which brand and
purpose.

## 24. Creative memory

Record strong carousel patterns: hero image style, dominant colour, type mode, slide
count, design device, CTA style, and how it performed. Don't turn winners into fixed
templates. The lesson is the principle, not the exact layout.

## 25. Final rule

A good carousel feels like turning the pages of a very short magazine. The first page
makes you open it, every page gives you something new, and the last page makes the next
step obvious. One story, several frames, no filler.
